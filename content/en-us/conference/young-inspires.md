Young Inspires was first introduced by PyCon Taiwan in 2021 as a dedicated program designed to create a space for teenagers to learn, share, and connect. Through this platform, we hope to enable Young Inspirers (youth speakers and participants) to share their experiences, ideas, challenges, and problem-solving methods throughout their learning journey — inspiring more youth to engage in technology and innovation.

At PyCon Taiwan 2026, we are honored to invite four outstanding young speakers on-site, focusing on four major areas: Go & algorithm applications, community earthquake early warning, self-taught cybersecurity practices, and gaming & tech community management. Through live interviews and practical sharing, they will present their project ideation, design, and implementation process — cultivating diverse thinking, cross-group collaboration, and system-integration skills, while fostering dialogue and information exchange across generations.

## Event Format

This year’s Young Inspires will be conducted in a live interview format for the first time. The event will take place over two days (Saturday and Sunday). On both days, there will be two sessions daily, with each session lasting 30 minutes — consisting of a 25-minute themed interview and a 5-minute live Q&A. During the themed interviews, a PyCon Taiwan host will converse with the speakers to explore their personal journeys, followed by an interactive Q&A session with the audience.

### From Go Player to Tech Maker: A Senior High Science Class Student Promoting Go Equity with Python

::prose-panel-speaker{name="Su Shang-Zhe" photo="/images/conference/young-inspires/su-shang-zhe.jpg" :compact="true"}
10th-grade student in the Science Class at Kaohsiung Senior High School and amateur 6-dan Go player. Self-taught in Python since junior high school, he refuses to be a mere "competition machine" solely chasing rankings. Realizing that existing Go self-study software was unfriendly to those without dedicated tutors, he decided to leverage Python for data analysis and software design to optimize learning tools and eliminate "unequal starting points," turning the Go board into a bridge that connects across socio-economic divides.
::

:conference-session-info{begin-time="2026-10-17T12:15:00+08:00" end-time="2026-10-17T12:45:00+08:00" language="ZHZH" location="R3"}

Holding an amateur 6-dan rank in Go, Su Shang-Zhe is currently a 10th-grade student in the Science Class at Kaohsiung Senior High School. He translates his strategic Go mindset into programming logic and problem-solving skills. Facing the dilemma of limited educational resources for self-learners and remote areas, he dedicated himself to Python data analysis and software optimization, aiming to bridge the urban-rural gap and remove financial barriers so that every learner passionate about Go can enjoy equal access to quality educational tools. This session will explore how he combined his Go expertise with programming skills to advocate for educational equity in Go.

::prose-panel-speaker{name="Kuo Chen-Yu" photo="/images/conference/young-inspires/kuo-chen-yu.jpg" :compact="true"}
Core member of the DPIP (Taiwan Earthquake Notification) team. Long dedicated to open-source communities and system development, he strives to popularize civil disaster prevention technology. Responsible for algorithm optimization and data integration within the project, he demonstrates the youth generation's passion and capability for open-source sharing and social responsibility.
::

:conference-session-info{begin-time="2026-10-17T12:50:00+08:00" end-time="2026-10-17T13:20:00+08:00" language="ZHZH" location="R3"}

### Building Asia’s Largest Minecraft Tech Community — The Community Management Philosophy of CloudTown

::prose-panel-speaker{name="Chiang Ching-Yu (FantasySakura0515)" photo="/images/conference/young-inspires/chiang-ching-yu.jpg" :compact="true"}
Student at Fu Jen Catholic University and founder of "CloudTown," Asia's largest Minecraft computer science and technology community. Since his sophomore year, he has participated in projects with medical and computer science research labs, possessing experience in low-level AI development and AI applications (RAG/MCP). He successfully extended his passion for Minecraft into computer engineering, establishing an invite-only community focused on innovation, IP protection, and mutual technical assistance that attracts thousands of players and developers.
::

Think Minecraft is just a game? A group of teenagers is taking it to a whole new level using computer science! This interview features Chiang Ching-Yu, founder of "CloudTown." He will share how he built a verified, vanilla-focused technical server with over 5,000 members from scratch, connected resources with university research labs, and integrated AI applications like RAG and MCP to show how passion can be transformed into a highly cohesive technical community.
